Margin of Safety Screener

Finds stocks trading below what three valuation models say they are worth, then ranks them by how wide the gap is and how cheap they look on earnings, cash flow and book value.

Footnote. Under each ticker, the green or red line says how far the stock beats or misses your active filters. Percentages such as revenue growth, ROE and margin of safety are compared in percentage points (pts). P/E, debt/equity and market cap are compared as a percent of the limit. Green cells meet the market cap or revenue growth filter, red cells miss it, and dimmed rows miss at least one filter. Open a row for the full filter check.

Results appear here as each stock loads. Every row shows where today's price sits against the estimated value: the tick marks fair value, and a bar that stops short of it means a discount.

How value is estimated, and what this can miss

Graham number. The square root of 22.5 × earnings per share × book value per share. It is the price at which a stock trades at 15× earnings and 1.5× book, Benjamin Graham's ceiling for a defensive investor.

Discounted cash flow. Free cash flow per share is grown for ten years, with the growth rate fading from the company's recent 5-year average toward the terminal rate, then discounted at your discount rate. The terminal value uses the standard growing perpetuity.

Graham growth formula. Earnings per share × (8.5 + 2 × growth) × 4.4 ÷ the AAA bond yield.

Estimated value is the median of the models that can run. Margin of safety is (value − price) ÷ value. Score is a 0–100 rank against the other stocks loaded: 40% margin of safety, 20% free cash flow yield, 20% earnings yield, 10% book value yield, 10% return on equity.

Limits. All three models lean on trailing numbers, so they flatter cyclical companies at a peak and punish growth companies that reinvest heavily. Banks and insurers need different models, since free cash flow is not meaningful for them. A low price is often a real problem, so read the flags on each row and check the filings before acting. This is a screening tool, not investment advice.
